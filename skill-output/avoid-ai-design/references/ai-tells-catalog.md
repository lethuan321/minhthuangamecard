## Visual checks beyond the scanner

- **L1: Generic page skeleton.** A centered hero, repeated cards, and standard footer may conceal the real product story. Recompose around actual content; keep familiar structures that aid usability.
- **L2: Flat hierarchy.** Similar weights, sizes, and spacing blur grouping. Fix relationships before adding decoration.
- **L3: Repetitive decoration.** Icon tiles, arrows, badges, gradients, or repeated reveals may dominate information. Remove effects that add no meaning.
- **L4: Uninformative imagery.** Generic mockups or stock imagery may obscure the product. Use authentic relevant assets or clear placeholders.
- **SD1: Cream/terracotta default.** Require a contextual reason for warmth and material references.
- **SD2: Near-black/lime default.** Require brand or domain justification for the high-contrast accent.
- **SD3: All-caps mono chrome.** Check readability and whether labels reflect genuine technical information.
- **SD4: Accented headline word.** Emphasize semantic importance rather than an arbitrary word.
- **SD5: Decorative numbering.** Use numbers for real steps, chronology, or indexed content.
- **X1: Design-system drift.** Compare tokens, navigation, type scale, and primitives across pages; centralize shared decisions.

Intentional teaching examples are exempt when explicitly marked or within illustrative paths. Review the surrounding real interface separately. Accessibility issues are actionable because of user impact, independently of any resemblance to generic design.
